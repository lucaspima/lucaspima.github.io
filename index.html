Research

I investigate how new words emerge in use and how word-formation patterns become organized into networks of linguistic knowledge.

My research combines corpus linguistics, computational methods, and statistical modelling to examine frequency, accessibility, and productivity in word formation, with a focus on compounds and blends.

Background

About.

I am Lucas Pivetta, a linguist and MA student at the University of São Paulo.

My research lies at the intersection of morphology, corpus linguistics, and cognitive and usage-based approaches. My MA project investigates how word-formation patterns emerge and expand in digital interactions, under the supervision of Paulo Roberto Gonçalves-Segundo.

I develop Python tools to identify neologisms, extract recurrent strings, and analyse morphological families in unannotated texts. I combine these procedures with statistical modelling and network analysis.

Education and other interests

I completed my undergraduate degree in Linguistics at the University of São Paulo and spent an exchange semester at Eduardo Mondlane University in Mozambique in 2024. There, I studied Southern Bantu morphology and typology, particularly xiChangana/xiTsonga, language contact, and orthographic standardization.

I am also interested in sign language morphophonology. My experience includes Brazilian Sign Language interpreting, language teaching, and training in quantitative methods for linguistic research.

Projects and methods

Portfolio.

Tools and methods developed as part of my research.

Python · Corpus linguistics

Identifying neologisms

An algorithm that extracts words from archived HTML comments and compares them against a lemmatized reference lexicon assembled from NLTK resources. The resulting candidates undergo manual validation.

Python · Morphology

Discovering word-formation patterns

Extraction of recurrent strings from unannotated text and grouping of words by shared material. This supports the identification of morphological families and candidate schemas across compounds and blends.

Longitudinal corpus · 2015–2025

Lexical innovation in digital interactions

Compilation and organization of a corpus of approximately 3.5 million words from 550 discussion threads. The analysis combines annual frequencies, mixed-effects models, quantile regression, principal components, and networks.
